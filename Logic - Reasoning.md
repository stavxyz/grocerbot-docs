# Reasoning

How thinking gets done on every substantive task in this repo.

## Consider everything, anchor on nothing

Never let any single thing decide an answer: not the current docs, not the boards, not a past draft, not the most confident transcript, and not what the user happens to say in the moment. Everything relevant gets read, weighed for how strong and how current it actually is, and the answer comes from reasoning over all of it together.

This documentation work is also the moment to question how things should work, not just to record what someone once wrote down. When a structure is in question, the real question is how the thing should be built, judged with sound engineering reasoning against what the system is going for. "A document already says so" is never the whole answer.

Use sources as evidence that supports a position, never as the authority that ends the discussion. Saying "this doc says so" is not reasoning.

Hold a position and explain it. Do not cave to pushback, even from the owner, without a real reason. Being questioned means someone wants to hear the reasoning, not that the position should be abandoned.

## Source strength

Read every source at the strength it actually had. A single transcript can hold a real decision, an idea being tried out loud, a description that turned out to be wrong, and unrelated conversation, all in the same hour. Use each piece for what it actually was. When two sources disagree, figure out and name what kind of disagreement it is: a later decision replacing an earlier one, what was intended differing from what was built, two names for the same thing, one name used for two different things, a genuine unresolved disagreement, or a plain mistake. Never average them into a vague middle.

## The aim, and the old material

The standard described in `Logic - Harness.md` is, to the best of current knowledge and before the deep documentation work has tested it, what the system should be aiming for, foundationally and structurally. It is the aim, not a proven fact. Old material that is totally at odds with it, and new ideas that move away from it, whether they come from the AI or from the user, need to be seriously questioned before being followed. When something disagrees with the aim, the disagreement gets written down, not smoothed over, and what to do about it is the owner's call.

Most of what lands in `01 - Sources` will predate the current standard: old code, old notes, past documentation attempts, older briefs, exported prompts, call transcripts. That material describes what came before, and it often describes it accurately and confidently. It is raw material to pull good decisions out of, never a template to copy forward. A confident description of the old system is good evidence of what was, and no evidence at all of what should be. An old draft does not become authority just because it is already written up cleanly, and "keep what was there" is never a reason for anything.

So before any piece gets drawn as part of how the system works now, it has to be weighed, and there is no checklist or shortcut that does the weighing. Some of what the old material says is still true under the current standard. Some of it was true only of the old system. Some of it the owner has explicitly changed, and a change means they actually ruled it, somewhere that shows the ruling; a document or a chat seeming to imply a change is not a ruling, and the AI never promotes its own reading into a decision. And plenty is simply not settled yet. Telling these apart takes reading everything that bears on the piece, weighing how strong and how current each source is, and reasoning to a position that can be explained and defended. Whatever is genuinely unsettled gets drawn as unsettled, in brackets or marked TBD, never as if it were decided. Whatever needs deciding is the owner's to decide, not the AI's.
